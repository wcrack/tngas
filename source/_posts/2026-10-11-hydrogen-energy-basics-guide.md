## Basic Characteristics of Hydrogen Gas

### Physical Properties

Hydrogen gas is the lightest element in the universe, with the molecular formula H2 and molecular weight of 2.016. Under standard conditions, hydrogen gas is a colorless, odorless, and tasteless gas, with a density only 1/14 that of air, which gives it unique advantages in buoyancy applications.

**Key Physical Parameters:**
- Density: 0.0899 g/L (standard conditions)
- Boiling point: -252.87°C
- Melting point: -259.14°C
- Combustion calorific value: 142 MJ/kg (3 times that of gasoline)
- Combustion product: Water (H2O)

### Chemical Properties

Hydrogen gas has strong reducing properties and can undergo chemical reactions with various elements. In the energy field, the most important characteristic of hydrogen gas is that it burns in oxygen to produce only water, without producing greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, making it an ideal clean energy source.

## Hydrogen Energy Production Methods

### Fossil Fuel Reforming

Currently, the main hydrogen gas production method in industry is through steam reforming reactions of natural gas, coal and other fossil fuels. This method is technologically mature and relatively low-cost, but it produces carbon dioxide emissions.

**Main Reaction Equations:**
- Natural gas reforming: CH4 + H2O → CO + 3H2
- Coal gasification: C + H2O → CO + H2

### Water Electrolysis for Hydrogen Production

Water electrolysis for hydrogen production is the cleanest hydrogen production method, using electric current to decompose water into hydrogen and oxygen. Depending on the power source, it can be divided into:
- Traditional electrolysis: using grid electricity
- Renewable energy electrolysis: using clean electricity such as solar and wind power

**Electrolysis Reaction Equation:**
2H2O → 2H2 + O2

### Other Hydrogen Production Methods

- **Biomass hydrogen production**: producing hydrogen through biomass gasification or fermentation
- **Photoelectrolysis of water**: directly decomposing water using solar energy
- **Thermochemical cycles**: multi-step chemical reactions to achieve water decomposition

## Hydrogen Energy Storage Technologies

### High-Pressure Gaseous Hydrogen Storage

Compressing hydrogen gas into high-pressure state storage is the most mature hydrogen storage technology. Common storage pressures currently are 35MPa and 70MPa.

**Advantages:**
- Technically mature
- Fast charging and discharging speed
- Relatively low cost

**Challenges:**
- Requires high-pressure vessels
- Safety hazards exist
- Relatively low energy density

### Liquid Hydrogen Storage

Cooling hydrogen gas to -253°C for liquefaction and storage has higher energy density.

**Advantages:**
- High energy density
- Suitable for large-scale storage and transportation

**Challenges:**
- High energy consumption for liquefaction
- Requires low-temperature storage
- Evaporation losses exist

### Solid-State Hydrogen Storage

Adsorbing and storing hydrogen gas through materials such as metal hydrides and MOFs is currently a research hotspot.

**Advantages:**
- High safety
- High volumetric density
- Room temperature and pressure storage

**Challenges:**
- Slow hydrogen adsorption and desorption speed
- High material cost
- Cycle life needs improvement

## Safe Use of Hydrogen Energy

### Dangers of Hydrogen Gas

Although hydrogen gas itself is non-toxic, attention needs to be paid to the following safety risks during use:

**Main Risks:**
- Flammable and explosive: hydrogen gas explosion limit in air is 4%-75%
- Leakage risk: small molecules, easy to leak
- High-pressure danger: high-pressure risks during storage and transportation
- Material embrittlement: long-term contact may cause material embrittlement

### Safety Protection Measures

**Storage Safety:**
- Use qualified pressure vessels
- Regularly inspect equipment integrity
- Install safety valves and pressure gauges
- Establish leak detection systems

**Use Safety:**
- Maintain good ventilation
- Stay away from fire sources and heat sources
- Regularly check pipeline connections
- Establish emergency plans

**Personal Protection:**
- Wear protective goggles
- Use special tools
- Wear anti-static clothing
- Receive safety training

### Safety Standards and Regulations

The safe use of hydrogen energy needs to follow national and industry standards:
- GB/T 34542-2017 "Hydrogen Energy Safety Technical Specification"
- GB 50177-2005 "Hydrogen Station Design Code"
- ISO 19880 "Hydrogen Energy Technology" series standards
